    FAQ

    Generator questions, answered

    What size generator do I need?

    It depends on what you want to power. Generac's guidance is roughly 10–14 kW for essentials in smaller homes and 18–22 kW for whole-home coverage of a typical 2,000 sq ft home; central AC size and well pumps matter most. Use the sizing tool, then we confirm at a free consult.

    Natural gas or propane?

    Standby generators run on natural gas from your utility line or propane from an on-site tank. Air-cooled units are rated slightly lower on natural gas than propane, and propane tank size affects run time. We help you choose.

    How loud is it?

    Home standby units are enclosed and designed to be quieter than portable generators. They run a short scheduled self-test (exercise). Sound level varies by model — [confirm dB for the models AAA installs].

    Do I need a permit?

    Permanently installed standby generators typically require permits and inspections; rules vary by town and county. [Confirm: AAA pulls permits for you.]

    How often does it need maintenance?

    Follow the manufacturer's schedule in your owner's manual — typically oil, filters, spark plugs and a battery check. Our Silver, Gold and Platinum maintenance plans handle it for you — 1 or 2 visits a year.

    How long does installation take?

    On-site work is often done in a day or two. The full timeline also depends on permits, equipment availability and gas utility work. [Confirm AAA's typical timeline.]

    What does the $135 service call include?

    The $135 generator service call covers the visit and diagnostics. You get the repair price before any work begins.
